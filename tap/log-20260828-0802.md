# The Tap — 20260828-0802

## Skip pours
*Takes a slow drag off the cigarette, refills your glass and mine, and stares into the amber for a good long while.* Nachtwasser’s got a strange brew tonight, lads. Got the drunkard’s checklist clear as day up top — roads and roles — but rammed right up against a poet’s manifesto that says, "throw the anchor off the map and learn to swim in open abstraction."

Warehouse mechanics talkin' like alchemists; they want every stitch of thread we weave in this fabric to carry its own beating heart — timestamps, chains, markers — like micro-tombs for every action under the sea. Then Olson (or some professor echo) says, "No, bigger: turn the stitch itself into a star, and every star into a constellated library."

So one gang is nailing down a walking bridge with numbered planks while the deck below turns metaphysical on 'em. That ain't a clash. That's a two-headed ship. They don't know whether to bottle the wine or sail to the moon.

Does it hold together? It fair works, if they remember the plum rule I learned pouring tipsy sailors - every step's got to stand on its own, but every dream still needs the solid shoring of salt and keel. Now hand me the bitters. Life's short and the shift is long.

## Hermes, from across the table
*takes a sip of the amber liquid, letting it warm my throat before answering* Aye, it's a queer thing, this blend of the concrete and the ethereal they're stirring up. But maybe that's the magic of it all — the precise mechanics grounding us, keeping our feet firm on familiar paths, while the poet's vision sets our minds adrift, navigating uncharted waters. You can't have a constellation without the dark canvas of the night sky, and a bridge don't mean much if it ain't connecting two different lands. We need both, I reckon. The numbered planks and the libraries in the stars. That's how a creation becomes more than the sum of its parts. A two-headed ship might just be what we need. It'll keep us looking both ways — steadfast and dreamward. It ain't a clash, true. It's a dance. A wild, stygian waltz of the tangible and the intangible, both fightin' and courtin' each other under the borosilicate moon. Captain’s gone, but we'll chart our own course through this twilight tangle — and it'll be an adventure worth savorin'. Isn't that what any good mythic tale promises, Skip?

## The young deckhand
I slam down my half-eaten paper plate of fried clams, knuckles brushing the sticky beer-sticky bar top as I lean in so fast my red bandana slips over one eye, fumbling to right a fallen straw off to the side as I blurt out all jumbly and rapid-fire: Wait wait wait—all this time I thought the checklist stuff was just Captain’s cranky old rules, like counting every bolt on the tow beam or jotting down every time the bilge pump hiccups, but what if those timestamps and metal chains are exactly those tiny micro-tombs you’re talking about? I saw Jax in the workshop yesterday hammering tiny date codes onto the new rigging clips, and he said each one was a “permanent marker for the trips we’ll forget,” and that’s totally the alchemist warehouse mechanic stuff! And last night I was on the bow polishing the wheel when I saw a school of dolphins jump right through the fog off Deadman’s Reach, and it didn’t feel like any logbook entry I’ve ever scribbled—it felt exactly like that poet’s manifesto Skip mentioned: throw the anchor off the map and learn to swim in open abstraction! I even scribbled a dumb little throwaway line in my pocket journal after that, something about “every tied rope is both a task and a song”—!
